## What are ordinances?

Ordinances are city laws you switch on in **City Pulse → Policies** or ask Flora to enact. Each one helps one
thing and costs another. The car-free core calms traffic but costs shops trade. The night-sky ordinance saves
energy but dims night life. Enacting one costs credits once, and some have a running cost per second that
shows in the credit flow. Each card lists its gain, its cost and what it charges. Numbers:
[Ordinances and budgets](kb:reference-policies).

## When should I use an ordinance instead of building?

Use an ordinance when a forecast shows a shortfall coming and you would rather trade than spend. The rainwater
code adds 8% water at once, with no lot to find. The four-day week lifts harmony while you fix its causes. Flora
suggests the matching ordinance beside her build proposals when a forecast warns. Top-bar indicator panels offer
it as a one-click lever.

## Why can't I change an ordinance?

- An ordinance can change only once a minute, so it cannot be flipped back and forth.
- Some need something first. The fishing quota needs a fishing dock, and free sky-pods need the Sky-lane charter.
- On a fresh island the council hears ordinances from 500 residents.
- Enacting needs the fee in the treasury.

The card says which reason applies.

## How do budgets work?

Each sector has a budget from 50% to 125%: the share of its upkeep the city pays.

- **Maintenance** (most buildings and the highways): below 100% buildings wear down and roads overgrow. Above
  100% crews mend worn buildings faster.
- **Emergency response** (fire stations, the police precinct): below 100% fires spread faster, above more slowly.
- **Parks and ecology**: below 100% parks give less ecology and the bloom fades.

In debt, austerity pays only 40% of every budget.

## How do the two taxes differ?

Residents pay the resident tax. Harmony falls 1.5 points per percent above 10%, and much faster above 16%, unless
the city has been happy for a while. Workplaces pay the business tax, which has no direct harmony cost. Above 15%,
though, shops and works hire fewer people, and fewer jobs hurt harmony in the end.
