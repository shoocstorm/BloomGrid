## Levels

A home or workplace that **thrives** (powered, watered, full or staffed, green and in good repair) grows a level
after a while, and again later. Each level holds more people and jobs and makes more, but costs more to run and
uses more power and water. You can also pay to upgrade (inspector, or ask Flora). Live numbers:
[Rules of play](kb:reference-rules).

## What a higher level looks like

Every building shows its level in its own way, so you can read a street at a glance. Homes add a storey and lift
their roof onto it, and at level 3 they gain a loft, a dormer or a glass garden room. Towers grow more segments and
a crown. Workshops and factories add lofts, decks, chimneys and gantries. Parks get a treehouse or a bandstand,
venues add stands, rides and canopies, and landmarks grow taller spires, halos and new wings. New additions start
clean; they weather with the rest of the building as it ages.

## Ageing

Every building ages. Its upkeep creeps up every 10 days (to +90%), it wears faster, and its walls weather. Under
austerity it ages twice as fast. **Renovate** (inspector) to reset its age and condition.

## Condition

Condition falls with age, floods, fire, quakes and austerity. Below 60% a building is *Worn*, below 35% *Neglected*.
Amenities only delight residents when they are kept in good repair.

## When to act

- Inventory `is:old` lists buildings worth renovating (their upkeep has crept up); `is:upgradable` those you can level.
- Renovate the most expensive old buildings first: the upkeep saving pays for it.
- Before upgrading many buildings, check you have power and water to spare.
