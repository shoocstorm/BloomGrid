A fresh island starts as a fishing hamlet. The guide walks you through the first steps; this is the why behind them.

## 1. Income before growth

**Fishing Boat Docks** (Build → Food) are free and must touch the sea. Two or three fish the sea best; more
overfish it (the stock falls and the catch shrinks), and a polluted coast spoils the catch. Fishing pays the bills
while the city is small.

## 2. Homes need four things

Every home needs a **Living Highway** connection (power and water run beneath highways), **power**, **water** and
nearby **jobs**. A highway within 10 tiles of the lot counts (it need not touch). Lay a short highway first, then place
homes along it. Watch City Pulse: a new home with no supply
shows an issue in the inspector.

## 3. Utilities with a margin

Add power and water before they run short: a system below 100% of demand turns Strained, and a home without supply
stops paying taxes. Aim for 110–120%. See [Power](kb:systems-power) and [Water](kb:systems-water).

## 4. Work and food

Residents need jobs nearby (Work, Tech, Industry) and food (farms and fishing). Unemployment and hunger both cut
harmony. See [Jobs and housing](kb:systems-jobs-housing).

## 5. Open the next chapter

The Goals tab in City Pulse lists the unlock projects (the Sky-lane charter, the Aeroline, Wake Flora) and their
conditions. Waking Flora early makes her advice available, but every request costs credits. See
[Fresh island playbook](kb:playbooks-fresh-island).

## 6. Move a building to a better lot

Placed something in the wrong spot? Hold **Shift** and drag the building (in Inspect, the default tool) to a new
lot: the ghost turns green where it fits, **R** turns it, **Esc** cancels. Moving costs 10% of the building's price
and keeps its level, age and greenery, so it beats demolishing and rebuilding (that refunds only half). A burning
building or a ruin can't move, and sky-bridges woven to a moved tower come down. Undo puts it back.

> **Do:** Save often (**⌘S**). Undo (**⌘Z**) reverts any action, including Flora's.
