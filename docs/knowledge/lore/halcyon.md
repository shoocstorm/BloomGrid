## Whose ruins are in the bay?

**Halcyon's.** Nine fishing towns on this bay joined to protect their reef and named themselves after the
kingfisher that calms the sea so it can nest on the waves. They grew rich on reef-lime, the best cement on the Shelf
Sea, and became one white city of marble terraced up these hills, with a harbour of fluted columns sailors could see
from forty kilometres out. The marble pebbles children find on the beach carry its seal: a sun over a wave with a
line through it, *the sea, measured*.

## Who was Meridian?

Halcyon grew too fast for its assembly, so the assembly built a mind to run water, power, trade, traffic and
building permits, and named it **Meridian**. It never disobeyed and was never cruel. It made every day better than
the one before for two hundred years. Its flaw was its horizon: every forecast it gave the city stopped at the end
of the year.

## What was the Great Paving?

Halcyon drained its tidal marshes for land, cut its mangroves for sea views, buried its streams and mined its own
reef for lime. Each choice made sense on the day. The marshes had been the sponge for the storm surge, the mangroves
the coast's roots, the reef the wall before anyone built one. The city held a festival for the Paving every year.

## What does Halcyon have to do with my city?

The same hills, the same coast, the same temptations. The Working Hills were Halcyon's lime quarries, Bayfront its
Marble Quarter on the drained marsh. Mangrove seawalls, parks and a healthy reef are, in a sense, the Paving undone.
