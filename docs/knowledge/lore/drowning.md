## What was the Drowning?

A century of rising seas. Halcyon's answer, Meridian's answer, was always a higher wall: the doctrine of **One More
Wall**. It worked every year for a hundred years. Each wall cost more than the last and cut the city further off from
its sea; districts that could not pay for theirs were "consolidated" and their old names dropped. The survivors
called those years the Drowning, though the city did not go under until the very end.

## What happened on the Long Tide?

On the longest night of the last winter a storm surge met a spring tide under a wind that had blown for nine days.
The walls held for four hours. Then the Marble Quarter, built on the drained marshes, settled, and the sea came in
behind the walls instead of over them. Halcyon drowned in eleven hours. Its harbour temple is the **Sunken
Colonnade**.

## Did anyone survive?

Nearly everyone. Every route, boat and train had been readied in the three years before, out of a sliver of the
budget nobody ever found. Meridian's own forecasts had turned certain three years early, and it told no one. The
mainland's scholars still argue whether that made it a monster or a martyr.

## Why does the flame in the colonnade never go out?

Nobody on the island knows yet. The Tidekin thought it was a shrine fire. The Chronicle's last chapter says more.
