## Who were the Tidekin?

The few thousand who stayed after the Long Tide: fishers, marsh-cutters, reef divers, people with nowhere else to go.
They lived in the old quarry terraces and on boats moored over their own streets. The mainland called them tidekin,
half an insult, and they kept it. They had no walls and no budget, only time and the reef. The fishing sayings you
hear in the harbour are theirs.

## What are the Three Promises?

The oldest law on the island, sworn by the reef-singers of the south-west shoals:

1. **Keep the reef healthy.** The reef was your wall before you built one.
2. **Take only what it can spare.** The sea gives every year to those who do not take it all at once.
3. **Never build where the water wants to be.** It was here first and will be here last.

In play they are ecology, fishing within its limits, and building back from the tide.

## How were the Tide Gates made?

Grown, not built. The reef-singers sang the promises to the shoals for a generation until three rings of living pearl
stood taller than any wave. The crown jewels came later, one per promise.

## Where did the Tidekin go?

Their young left for the mainland. **Ilse Marrow**, the last singer on the island, sang the Gates to sleep and joined
the Drift. Every Drift flotilla still carries a singer, and every Drift child still learns the Three Promises.
